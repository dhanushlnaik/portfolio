## How I got in

I started at **EtherWorld** writing about Ethereum. Having to explain things like EIPs and consensus to readers made me understand them much better than reading about them did.

Later I joined **Avarch LLC** as a developer, working on Ethereum products like EIPsInsight.

## 1. Ignore the hype

There's a lot of noise in crypto. The interesting part is how these systems let people trust each other without a middleman, and the useful work is building tools around that.

## 2. You'll spend more time communicating than coding

Most Ethereum work happens in distributed teams across timezones. You get good at writing things down, keeping git history clean and reading long specs.

## 3. Make things easy to check

Working on public infrastructure made me care about transparency. If people can't check what your system is doing, they shouldn't have to trust it. I now apply that to every backend I build, blockchain or not.
